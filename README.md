## 5. Freedom of Expression and Digital Communication

Digital technologies have expanded public communication, but they have also created complex political and legal challenges.

### Major Issues

- Online censorship.
- Content moderation.
- Disinformation.
- Misinformation.
- Hate speech.
- Political manipulation.
- Online harassment.
- Doxxing.
- Surveillance of journalists.
- Platform power.
- Government pressure.
- Algorithmic amplification.
- Shadow banning.
- Digital propaganda.
- Internet shutdowns.

### Analytical Balance

Research should avoid treating freedom of expression as unlimited or treating content moderation as automatically legitimate.

A balanced analysis should ask:

1. What expression is being restricted?
2. Who is restricting it?
3. Under what legal authority?
4. Is the restriction necessary?
5. Is it proportionate?
6. Is the rule applied consistently?
7. Is there an appeal process?
8. Are political minorities disproportionately affected?
9. Does the measure protect people from harm?
10. Does the measure create a chilling effect?

## Phase 1: Define the problem

- Identify the technology.
- Identify the institution or actor.
- Identify affected communities.
- Define the political or human-rights issue.
- Formulate research questions.

## Phase 2: Map the institutional context

- Identify relevant laws.
- Identify regulatory institutions.
- Identify international standards.
- Identify companies and public bodies.
- Identify oversight mechanisms.
- Identify available remedies.

## Phase 3: Review the literature

- Locate academic research.
- Review legal sources.
- Examine policy reports.
- Identify methodological debates.
- Compare competing interpretations.
- Map gaps in the literature.

## Phase 4: Assess rights impacts

- Identify potentially affected rights.
- Examine necessity and proportionality.
- Analyze discrimination risks.

# Human-Rights Impact Assessment Framework

Before deploying or studying an AI system, researchers and institutions should examine:

| Area | Assessment questions |
|---|---|
| Purpose | What problem is the system intended to address? |
| Authority | Who authorized its development or deployment? |
| Legal basis | What law, policy, or institutional rule applies? |
| Necessity | Is the system necessary for the stated purpose? |
| Proportionality | Is the interference with rights proportionate? |
| Data | What data are collected, used, and retained? |
| Quality | Are the data accurate, representative, and reliable? |
| Bias | Could the system discriminate against particular groups? |
| Transparency | Can affected persons understand the system’s operation? |
| Explainability | Can decisions be meaningfully explained? |
| Human control | Is meaningful human oversight available? |
| Accountability | Who is responsible for errors, misuse, or harm? |
| Remedy | Can affected persons challenge or appeal decisions? |
| Security | How are the system and data protected? |
| Participation | Were affected communities consulted? |
| Accessibility | Can people with different abilities and languages use or challenge the system? |
| Environment | What are the energy and ecological consequences? |
| Review | Will the system be independently and regularly evaluated? |
| Exit | Can the system be suspended or discontinued if harms occur? |

# Suggested Policy Principles

## Human Dignity

AI systems should respect the inherent dignity and agency of every person.

## Human Rights by Design

Human-rights considerations should be integrated into the design, development, procurement, deployment, and review of AI systems.

## Human Oversight

Important decisions affecting rights should not be left entirely to automated systems without meaningful human review.

## Transparency

Individuals and communities should receive understandable information about the purpose, operation, risks, and limitations of high-impact AI systems.

## Accountability

Organizations deploying AI should remain responsible for foreseeable harms caused by their systems.

## Non-Discrimination

AI systems should be assessed for discriminatory effects across gender, caste, race, religion, disability, language, migration status, and other relevant categories.

## Privacy Protection

Data collection should be lawful, necessary, proportionate, secure, purpose-limited, and subject to oversight.

## Explainability

Affected persons should receive meaningful explanations when automated systems significantly affect their rights or opportunities.

## Access to Remedy

People affected by automated decisions should have access to notification, correction, review, appeal, and effective remedy.

## Democratic Participation

Technology governance should include affected communities, civil society, researchers, public institutions, and independent experts.

## Environmental Responsibility

AI governance should account for energy use, environmental impacts, resource consumption, and the distribution of ecological costs.

## Global Inclusion

AI standards should include the perspectives, capacities, needs, and priorities of countries and communities in the Global South.

# AI, Human Rights, and International Norms

This research portfolio is informed by emerging international approaches that connect AI with:

- Human dignity.
- Fundamental rights.
- Democracy.
- Rule of law.
- Equality.
- Transparency.
- Accountability.
- Human oversight.
- Environmental protection.
- International cooperation.

UNESCO’s Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence presents a global ethical framework applicable across AI development and use, emphasizing human rights, dignity, fairness, transparency, accountability, sustainability, and human oversight. [113][114][117]

The Council of Europe Framework Convention on AI is designed to ensure that activities across the AI life cycle remain consistent with human rights, democracy, and the rule of law. It also uses a risk-sensitive approach and provides for follow-up through a Conference of the Parties. [116][120]

The OHCHR works on digital technologies and AI through rights-based guidance, including support for regulation and oversight of AI systems. [122]
