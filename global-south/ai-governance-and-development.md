# AI Governance and Development in the Global South

## Research Focus

AI governance should address the different technological, economic, legal, institutional, and social conditions of countries in the Global South.

A global framework should not assume that every country has:

- Equal digital infrastructure.
- Equal access to computing capacity.
- Equal regulatory institutions.
- Equal technical expertise.
- Equal financial resources.
- Equal representation in standard-setting.
- Equal control over data.
- Equal access to digital skills.

---

## Core Issues

- Technological dependency.
- Unequal digital infrastructure.
- Data colonialism.
- Digital divides.
- Language exclusion.
- Limited regulatory capacity.
- Foreign technology dependence.
- Data extraction.
- AI labour inequalities.
- Environmental costs.
- Inadequate representation.
- Unequal access to remedy.
- Public-sector capacity constraints.

---

## Research Questions

1. Who controls AI infrastructure?
2. Who owns and processes data?
3. Are developing countries rule-makers or rule-takers?
4. How can local knowledge be protected?
5. Does AI deepen global inequality?
6. How can technology transfer be made fair?
7. How can AI support inclusive development?
8. Are Global South communities represented in international AI standards?
9. What forms of digital sovereignty are possible?
10. How can development and human rights be balanced?

## Policy Priorities

- Build local technical capacity.
- Support multilingual AI systems.
- Protect community data.
- Strengthen privacy and accountability.
- Include Global South voices in international standard-setting.
- Promote technology transfer.
- Avoid extractive data practices.
- Evaluate environmental impacts.
- Improve public-sector expertise.
- Create accessible remedies.
- Protect vulnerable communities.
- Link AI governance with development and social justice.
