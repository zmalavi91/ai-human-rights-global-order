# Research Themes

## Dr. Zaid Mustafa Alavi

This document maps the principal research themes developed in this repository. The themes connect artificial intelligence, digital technologies, human rights, International Relations, international law, global governance, security, democracy, and social justice.

---

## Research Architecture

My research examines emerging technologies through four connected levels:

| Level | Central concern |
|---|---|
| Individual | Privacy, dignity, autonomy, equality, and freedom |
| Institutional | Law, regulation, accountability, and public administration |
| National | Security, governance, sovereignty, and development |
| International | Global order, power, norms, cooperation, and conflict |

The central analytical question is:

> How do emerging technologies reshape political power, human-rights protection, institutional accountability, and international order?

# Theme 1: Artificial Intelligence and International Relations

## Focus

This theme examines how artificial intelligence changes the conduct of international politics and affects the distribution of power among states, international organizations, technology companies, and civil society.

## Key Areas

- AI-enabled foreign-policy analysis.
- Strategic competition.
- International security.
- Military technology.
- Diplomatic decision-making.
- Economic statecraft.
- Technology transfer.
- Digital infrastructure.
- Strategic autonomy.
- AI diplomacy.
- International norm development.
- Global technological inequality.

## Core Questions

1. How is AI changing the distribution of international power?
2. How are states using AI in foreign policy and security?
3. Does AI increase the strategic advantage of technologically advanced states?
4. What role do private technology companies play in international politics?
5. How does AI affect sovereignty and strategic autonomy?
6. Can AI governance become a new area of international diplomacy?
7. How can developing countries participate meaningfully in global AI governance?

## Methods

- Foreign-policy analysis.
- Comparative case studies.
- Strategic and geopolitical analysis.
- Institutional analysis.
- International-norm analysis.
- Policy-document analysis.

## Possible Outputs

- Research articles.
- Policy briefs.
- Conference papers.
- Strategic assessments.
- Comparative country studies.
- Teaching modules on AI and International Relations.

# Theme 2: AI Governance and Global Order

## Focus

AI governance refers to the laws, institutions, standards, principles, and practices that guide the development and use of artificial intelligence.

This theme examines how governance systems can balance innovation, security, human rights, accountability, and democratic participation.

## Key Areas

- National AI regulation.
- International AI standards.
- Global governance.
- Regulatory institutions.
- Corporate responsibility.
- Public-sector AI.
- AI safety and security.
- Human-rights impact assessment.
- Algorithmic accountability.
- International cooperation.
- Global South participation.
- Technology and democracy.

## Core Principles

- Human dignity.
- Human rights.
- Human oversight.
- Transparency.
- Explainability.
- Accountability.
- Non-discrimination.
- Privacy.
- Safety.
- Security.
- Inclusion.
- Democratic participation.
- Environmental sustainability.
- Access to remedy.

UNESCO’s Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence connects responsible AI with human rights, dignity, fairness, transparency, accountability, sustainability, and human oversight. [113][117][119]

The Council of Europe’s AI framework connects the governance of AI with human rights, democracy, and the rule of law. [116][120]

## Core Questions

1. Which institutions should regulate high-impact AI?
2. How can AI systems be made transparent and explainable?
3. Who is responsible when automated systems cause harm?
4. What forms of international cooperation are required?
5. How can AI governance include developing countries?
6. How can democratic oversight be protected?
7. What standards should apply across the AI life cycle?

## Possible Outputs

- AI-governance frameworks.
- Regulatory comparisons.
- Institutional accountability maps.
- Human-rights impact assessments.
- Policy briefs.
- International-governance analyses.

# Theme 3: Digital Surveillance and Human Rights

## Focus

This theme analyzes digital surveillance as a political, legal, technological, and human-rights issue.

Surveillance can serve legitimate public purposes, but it can also enable arbitrary monitoring, political repression, discrimination, and restrictions on civic freedoms.

## Rights at Stake

- Privacy.
- Freedom of expression.
- Freedom of association.
- Freedom of movement.
- Political participation.
- Equality.
- Human dignity.
- Freedom of religion.
- Protection of journalists.
- Protection of human-rights defenders.
- Access to remedy.

## Key Areas

- Mass surveillance.
- Biometric identification.
- Facial recognition.
- Location tracking.
- Communications monitoring.
- Predictive policing.
- Digital profiling.
- Internet monitoring.
- Social-media surveillance.
- Surveillance of minorities.
- Surveillance of activists and journalists.
- Data retention.
- Cross-border data sharing.

## Core Questions

1. Is surveillance authorized by law?
2. Is the purpose legitimate?
3. Is surveillance necessary?
4. Is it proportionate?
5. Who is being monitored?
6. What data are collected?
7. Who can access the data?
8. How long are data retained?
9. Is independent oversight available?
10. Can affected people challenge surveillance?
11. Are vulnerable communities disproportionately affected?
12. Does surveillance create a chilling effect on speech and participation?

## Methods

- Legal analysis.
- Human-rights impact assessment.
- Policy analysis.
- Institutional accountability analysis.
- Critical discourse analysis.
- Comparative case studies.

## Possible Outputs

- Surveillance-risk assessments.
- Digital-rights policy briefs.
- Human-rights reports.
- Case studies.
- Legal and institutional summaries.
- Classroom exercises.

# Theme 5: Automated Decision-Making and Algorithmic Accountability

## Focus

This theme examines decisions made fully or partly through algorithms, AI systems, predictive models, or data-driven tools.

## Areas of Application

- Welfare administration.
- Public-benefit distribution.
- Immigration.
- Refugee processing.
- Employment.
- Education.
- Healthcare.
- Credit assessment.
- Policing.
- Criminal justice.
- Border management.
- National security.

## Human-Rights Risks

- Discrimination.
- Inaccurate data.
- Lack of explanation.
- Denial of due process.
- Responsibility gaps.
- Unreviewable decisions.
- Exclusion from public services.
- Unequal error rates.
- Limited appeal rights.
- Automated profiling.

## Accountability Questions

1. What decision is automated?
2. Why is automation used?
3. What data are used?
4. Who designed the system?
5. Who approved deployment?
6. Has the system been independently tested?
7. Are certain communities disproportionately affected?
8. Is human review meaningful?
9. Can individuals appeal?
10. Who is legally responsible for harm?
11. Can the system be suspended?
12. Are audit records publicly available?

## Possible Outputs

- Algorithmic accountability checklists.
- Automated-decision case studies.
- Equality-impact assessments.
- Regulatory analysis.
- Public-sector governance frameworks.
- Policy recommendations.

# Theme 6: Freedom of Expression and Digital Communication

## Focus

Digital platforms have expanded public participation and communication while creating new challenges involving censorship, disinformation, hate speech, political manipulation, harassment, and platform power.

## Key Areas

- Content moderation.
- Online censorship.
- Disinformation.
- Misinformation.
- Hate speech.
- Political manipulation.
- Online harassment.
- Digital propaganda.
- Platform governance.
- Internet shutdowns.
- Journalistic freedom.
- Political speech.
- Algorithmic amplification.

## Core Questions

1. What expression is being restricted?
2. Who is restricting it?
3. What legal authority applies?
4. Is the restriction necessary?
5. Is the restriction proportionate?
6. Is the rule applied consistently?
7. Is there a transparent appeal process?
8. Are minorities or political opponents disproportionately affected?
9. Does content moderation protect people from harm?
10. Does regulation create a chilling effect?

## Analytical Balance

Research should recognize both:

- The need to protect individuals and communities from violence, abuse, and manipulation.
- The need to prevent censorship, political repression, and arbitrary restrictions on public debate.

## Possible Outputs

- Digital-speech policy briefs.
- Content-moderation analysis.
- Freedom-of-expression case studies.
- Platform-accountability frameworks.
- Human-rights assessments.

## Key Areas

- Autonomous weapons.
- Target-recognition systems.
- Battlefield surveillance.
- Predictive military systems.
- Cyber operations.
- Civilian-risk assessment.
- Humanitarian data.
- Digital infrastructure attacks.
- Algorithmic targeting.
- Human control over military systems.

## International Humanitarian Law Questions

1. Can the system distinguish civilians from combatants?
2. Can it apply proportionality?
3. Can it observe precautions in attack?
4. Who makes the final decision?
5. Who is responsible for errors?
6. Can decisions be reconstructed after an incident?
7. Can victims access justice?
8. Does automation reduce or increase civilian risk?
9. Can meaningful human control be maintained?
10. Does the technology lower the threshold for using force?

## Humanitarian Concerns

- Civilian protection.
- Displacement.
- Infrastructure damage.
- Humanitarian access.
- Protection of aid workers.
- Documentation of violations.
- Protection of medical and civilian data.
- Accountability for automated harm.

## Possible Outputs

- Conflict-technology policy briefs.

# Theme 8: Gender, Inequality, and AI

## Focus

This theme examines how AI and digital systems may reproduce or intensify gender, caste, racial, class, disability, linguistic, and geographical inequalities.

## Key Areas

- Gender bias in datasets.
- Automated employment discrimination.
- Facial-recognition disparities.
- Gender-based online violence.
- Surveillance of women activists.
- Digital exclusion.
- Reproductive and health-data privacy.
- Representation in AI institutions.
- Caste and algorithmic discrimination.
- Disability and accessibility.
- Language exclusion.

## Core Questions

1. Are women and gender minorities represented in datasets?
2. Does the system reproduce stereotypes?
3. Are error rates different across groups?
4. Does automated decision-making affect employment or welfare access?
5. How does online violence restrict political participation?
6. Are diverse groups involved in technology design?
7. Are gender-sensitive remedies available?
8. How do gender, caste, race, class, and disability intersect?

## Analytical Approach

An intersectional approach examines overlapping forms of disadvantage rather than treating social groups as internally uniform.

## Possible Outputs

- Gender-impact assessments.
- Intersectional technology research.
- Digital-violence policy briefs.
- Equality audits.
- Feminist technology analysis.
- Research on caste and AI in South Asia.
