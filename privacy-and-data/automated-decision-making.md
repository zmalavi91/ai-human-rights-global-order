# Automated Decision-Making and Human Rights

## Overview

Automated decision-making refers to decisions that are made fully or partly through computational systems, algorithms, artificial intelligence, or data-driven models.

These systems may be used in:

- Welfare administration.
- Immigration.
- Refugee processing.
- Employment.
- Education.
- Healthcare.
- Credit scoring.
- Policing.
- Criminal justice.
- Public benefits.
- Border control.
- National security.

---

## Human-Rights Concerns

Automated decisions may affect:

- Equality.
- Non-discrimination.
- Privacy.
- Due process.
- Human dignity.
- Freedom of expression.
- Access to public services.
- Right to an effective remedy.
- Political participation.

---

## Assessment Questions

1. What decision is being automated?
2. Why is automation being used?
3. What data are used?
4. Are the data accurate?
5. Are the data representative?
6. Who designed the model?
7. Who approved its use?
8. Has the system been independently tested?
9. Are different groups affected differently?
10. Is a human review available?
11. Can individuals appeal?
12. Can decisions be explained?
13. Who is legally responsible?
14. What happens when the system makes an error?
15. Can the system be suspended or withdrawn?

---

## Procedural Safeguards

A high-impact automated system should include:

- Prior impact assessment.
- Notice to affected persons.
- Meaningful explanation.
- Human review.
- Correction of inaccurate data.
- Appeal procedures.
- Independent oversight.
- Audit records.
- Data security.
- Periodic review.
- Suspension mechanisms.

---

## Bias Assessment

Potential sources of bias include:

- Historical discrimination.
- Incomplete data.
- Underrepresentation.
- Proxy variables.
- Linguistic limitations.
- Institutional assumptions.
- Unequal access to technology.
- Poorly designed categories.
- Data-labeling errors.
- Lack of community consultation.

---

## Accountability Chain

| Stage | Responsible actor | Accountability question |
|---|---|---|
| Design |  | Was the system designed responsibly? |
| Data collection |  | Were data lawfully and fairly collected? |
| Training |  | Were data representative and accurate? |
| Testing |  | Were risks and unequal effects examined? |
| Deployment |  | Was deployment authorized and monitored? |
| Decision |  | Can the decision be explained and reviewed? |
| Appeal |  | Can affected persons challenge the decision? |
| Remedy |  | Who corrects harm and provides redress? |

---

## Policy Principles

- No high-impact decision should be entirely unreviewable.
- Individuals should be informed when automation significantly affects them.
- Human oversight should be meaningful rather than merely formal.
- Systems should be independently audited.
- Discriminatory outcomes should trigger investigation.
- Affected persons should have access to remedy.
- Responsibility should not be transferred to an algorithm.
