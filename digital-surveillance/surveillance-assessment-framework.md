# Digital Surveillance Assessment Framework

## Purpose

This framework is designed to assess the legal, political, ethical, and human-rights implications of digital surveillance systems.

It can be used for:

- Academic research.
- Policy analysis.
- Human-rights impact assessment.
- Institutional review.
- Classroom exercises.
- Civil-society monitoring.
- Public-policy recommendations.

---

## 1. System Identification

- Name of the surveillance system.
- Institution or organization responsible.
- Technology used.
- Date of deployment.
- Geographical scope.
- Categories of people affected.
- Stated purpose.
- Actual or suspected uses.
- Public or private operators.
- Connection with other databases or systems.

---

## 2. Legal Authority

Assess:

- Constitutional authority.
- Statutory authority.
- Regulatory framework.
- Judicial authorization.
- Administrative authorization.
- Emergency powers.
- Data-protection obligations.
- Human-rights obligations.
- International-law requirements.

### Questions

- Is surveillance authorized by law?
- Is the law publicly accessible?
- Is the legal authority sufficiently precise?
- Are there independent safeguards?
- Can affected persons challenge the surveillance?
- Are remedies available?

---

## 3. Legitimate Purpose

Possible stated purposes include:

- National security.
- Crime prevention.
- Public safety.
- Counterterrorism.
- Border management.
- Public health.
- Fraud prevention.
- Service delivery.
- Cybersecurity.

### Critical Questions

- Is the stated purpose legitimate?
- Is surveillance being used for another purpose?
- Is the purpose sufficiently specific?
- Could the system be used for political monitoring?
- Are the objectives consistent with human rights?

---

## 4. Necessity and Proportionality

Assess whether:

- Surveillance is necessary.
- Less intrusive alternatives exist.
- The measure is proportionate.
- The duration is limited.
- Data collection is minimized.
- The scope is justified.
- The benefits outweigh the rights-related risks.

---

## 5. Data Assessment

Document:

- Types of data collected.
- Source of data.
- Sensitive data collected.
- Biometric data.
- Location data.
- Communications data.
- Health data.
- Political or religious information.
- Data retention period.
- Data accuracy.
- Data-sharing arrangements.
- Cross-border transfers.
- Deletion procedures.

## 7. Discrimination and Bias

Assess possible effects on:

- Gender.
- Caste.
- Race.
- Religion.
- Language.
- Disability.
- Migration status.
- Political identity.
- Economic status.
- Region.
- Age.

### Questions

- Was the data representative?
- Were marginalized groups included?
- Were historical inequalities reproduced?
- Were error rates compared across groups?
- Was independent bias testing conducted?
- Can affected persons challenge discriminatory outcomes?

---

## 8. Oversight

Identify:

- Internal oversight.
- Parliamentary oversight.
- Judicial oversight.
- Independent regulators.
- Data-protection authorities.
- Human-rights institutions.
- Civil-society monitoring.
- Public reporting requirements.
- Audit procedures.

### Oversight Questions

- Who monitors the system?
- Is oversight independent?
- Are audits regular?
- Are audit results public?
- Can oversight bodies access technical information?
- Are violations investigated?
- Are officials held responsible?

---

## 9. Transparency

Assess whether the public knows:

- Why surveillance is used.
- What data are collected.
- Who operates the system.
- How long data are retained.
- Who can access the data.
- How data are shared.
- How errors are corrected.
- How complaints are handled.
- How the system is audited.

---

## 10. Remedy and Redress

Affected persons should be able, where appropriate, to:

- Know that they have been affected.
- Obtain relevant information.
- Correct inaccurate information.
- Challenge decisions.
- Request review.
- Complain to an independent body.
- Seek judicial remedy.
- Receive compensation or other redress where legally available.

---

## 11. Vulnerable Communities

Identify possible effects on:

- Human-rights defenders.
- Journalists.
- Political opposition.
- Religious minorities.
- Ethnic minorities.
- Refugees.
- Migrants.
- Women.
- Children.
- LGBTQ+ persons.
- Persons with disabilities.
- Low-income communities.
- Linguistic minorities.

---

## 12. Risk Classification

### Low Risk

Limited data, narrow purpose, strong safeguards, independent review, and effective remedy.

### Medium Risk

Significant personal data, moderate uncertainty, incomplete oversight, or limited transparency.

### High Risk

Sensitive data, biometric surveillance, predictive profiling, political monitoring, weak oversight, unclear legal authority, or limited remedy.

### Prohibited or Unacceptable Risk

Surveillance that facilitates persecution, systematic discrimination, arbitrary political repression, or serious and unremedied violations of fundamental rights.

---

## 13. Recommendations

- Define a specific legal purpose.
- Minimize data collection.
- Limit retention.
- Require independent authorization.
- Conduct regular human-rights impact assessments.
- Publish transparency reports.
- Establish independent oversight.
- Provide notice and appeal mechanisms.
- Test for discriminatory impacts.
- Protect journalists and human-rights defenders.
- Create clear deletion procedures.
- Suspend systems that create serious unremedied harm.
