# AI and Global Governance: A Human-Rights Framework

## Executive Summary

Artificial intelligence is becoming increasingly important in public administration, security, economic competition, international politics, and social communication. Its benefits may include improved research, public services, health systems, and decision support. However, high-impact AI systems may also create risks involving privacy, discrimination, surveillance, misinformation, accountability, and unequal access. Effective governance should combine innovation with human-rights safeguards, transparency, independent oversight, meaningful human responsibility, and accessible remedies.

## Policy Problem

AI governance is difficult because technological systems may operate across borders while regulation remains primarily national. Different countries may apply different standards, and private companies may control systems with significant social and political consequences.

## Key Risks

- Privacy violations.
- Discriminatory outcomes.
- Lack of transparency.
- Automated political manipulation.
- Digital exclusion.
- Unclear responsibility.
- Excessive surveillance.
- Security vulnerabilities.
- Unequal technological capacity.
- Weak access to remedy.

## Policy Recommendations

1. Require human-rights impact assessments for high-impact AI systems.
2. Establish independent oversight authorities.
3. Require transparency about system purpose, data, and risks.
4. Provide accessible mechanisms for explanation and appeal.
5. Prohibit discriminatory applications.
6. Protect privacy through data minimization and security.
7. Include affected communities in governance decisions.
8. Strengthen international cooperation.
9. Support developing countries in building responsible AI capacity.
10. Review high-impact systems regularly.

## Conclusion

AI governance should not be treated solely as a technical or commercial issue. It is also a question of rights, power, accountability, democracy, and international order.
